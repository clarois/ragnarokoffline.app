# Settings: Mods

Three lists, **Installed**, **Updates** and **Find Mods**, under a header that
stays at the top while you scroll. The header also holds **Add mod from
folder…**, **Add UI skin…** and **Open mods folder**. A bar at the bottom of the
window holds **Apply & restart server** and says what is waiting for it.

## Installed

One line per mod: its switch, its name and version, badges, and the first line
of its description. Click the name to open the rest — the whole description,
who made it, its options, and **Remove…**. The switch only switches; it never
opens the row.

The list is in three groups: **Needs attention** (an update waiting, a mod the
app would not load, or one whose tables the server could not read), mods
**installed by you**, and mods **included with the app**. The badges say the
same at a glance: **GitHub**, **Update v1 → v2**, **Not loaded**, **Problem**,
**Host service on/off**, and **Not applied** while a change to that mod waits
for Apply.

Two things are said in text the mod did not write:

- **"Changes which commands players can use."** A mod supplying `groups.yml` or
  `atcommands.yml` decides what every player may do. That is worth knowing from
  the app rather than from the mod's own description.
- **"Not loaded — …"** A refused mod is not one you can switch on, so its
  switch is greyed out rather than pretending otherwise. The reason is given: usually it
  wants a newer app version, or an era you are not running.

Mods shipped with the app are marked **Included**. They can be switched off, and
replaced by installing a mod of the same name, but not deleted.

## Mod settings

A mod can declare its own options — a checkbox, a number, a short piece of text
— and they appear in its row, under **Options**. The two bundled control mods use this to let
you hide their on-screen buttons while keeping what they do.

Options are read when the client configuration is regenerated, so **Apply**
first and the game picks them up on its next load. Changing one does not turn
the mod off.

Some mods have a **Settings…** button instead. Those mods ship a settings window
of their own, which can arrange the options its own way and has its own button
to apply them. That window can only change that mod's options and restart the
server; it cannot reach the internet or anything else on your computer.

## Apply

**Apply & restart server** applies every switch and every option at once, then
restarts the server. Until you do, the bar at the bottom says how many changes
are waiting and lists them — a mod switched on or off, an option changed, an
installed or updated mod that is on, a removed mod that was on — and Apply is
highlighted. It also says whether the game will need reopening.

**Discard** puts the switches and options back as they were last applied. An
install, a removal or an update has already changed the files, so Discard
leaves those for Apply.

A mod with files the game window loads (`data/`, `System/`, `BGM/` or
`client/`) needs the game reloaded. After an Apply that changed one, the bar
says so, with a **Reopen game** button when the game window is open (or **Open
game** when it is not); the line goes away once the game has loaded again,
however you reopened it. After an Apply that only touched the server, the bar
says the game does not need reopening.

## Find mods

The **Find Mods** list browses the mod list: mods reviewed into the app's repository by
pull request. Most are a folder there. The ones marked **from GitHub** live in
their author's own repository instead, and installing one installs that
repository's latest release. The app shows you the repository, the release and
what kind of code it carries before anything is installed.

Search by name, author or what a mod does, or click one of the tags above the
list. A mod you already have is marked **✓ Installed** and has **Manage**,
which opens its row in Installed (or **Update…** when there is one).

## Updates

For a mod installed **from GitHub**, opening the Mods tab or pressing **Check
for updates** (in **Updates**) asks GitHub whether there is a newer release.
Every mod with one is listed in **Updates**, with the release notes a click
away, and a red number on **Mods** and **Updates** says how many. Nothing updates by itself: **Update…** shows the new release first, and
only replaces the installed copy if you say yes. Your options for the mod, and
whether it is switched on, are kept, and the new files load at the next
**Apply**. If the new release needs a newer app, the update is refused and the
version you have stays.

The list vouches for the mod's repository, not for each release, so its author
can publish a new version without review. That is what the dialog says, and
why updating is your click and not the app's.

## Install a mod

**Add mod from folder…** takes a folder, a `.zip` or a `.rar`. The file's content decides which it is, not its name. A `.rar` needs `bsdtar`, which macOS and Windows have built in; on Linux, install `libarchive-tools` (or unpack it and choose the folder).

**A mod is not data.** It drops scripts and tables into the server's paths and
can ship JavaScript the game page executes. Installing one is running somebody
else's code, so the app checks before it moves anything and unpacks defensively
— but the judgement about whether to trust it is yours.

## Remove a mod

Mods you installed have a **Remove…** button in their row. It asks first, then moves the
mod's folder to the system trash — so a wrong click can be undone from there —
and forgets whether it was switched on and what its options were, so installing
it again starts from its own defaults. Characters and items are not touched.

A removed mod that was switched on stays in the running server until the next
**Apply**. Mods marked **included** have no Remove button; switch them off
instead.

## Open mods folder

Installed mods live in `state/mods`, deliberately outside the part of the tree
an update replaces. Adding a folder there by hand works as well as installing
one.

See [Making mods](./modding) to write your own.
